I trained as an industrial and mechanical engineer at the University of Stellenbosch, and that
training shaped everything since. Engineering teaches you that most problems presented as
judgement calls are actually measurement problems — you're arguing about opinions because nobody
has established the facts. I've found that to be true of almost every sourcing decision and every
struggling technology programme I've worked on.

My career has run across two connected tracks. One is facilities services sourcing: cleaning, food
services, and maintenance contracts for corporate, industrial, financial, energy, and government
organisations in the United States, the United Kingdom, and internationally. The other is workplace
and maintenance technology — leading the functional delivery of enterprise IWMS and CMMS
implementations across operations and maintenance, space and moves, reservations, projects, and
lease administration.

Those two tracks are why SynPro looks the way it does. Having run sourcing processes, I know what a
well-structured one is worth. Having delivered implementations, I know what a client can't see from
their side of the table.

## Career

Before founding SynPro I worked in consulting and advisory roles — a corporate real estate
technology consultancy, and earlier management consulting practices delivering enterprise systems
and facilities programmes for blue-chip organisations across multiple sectors and countries.

Several of those client relationships ran six years or more, through multiple contract renewals.
That's the part of the record I'd point to first. Anyone can win an engagement; staying useful for
six years is a different test.

## Why SynPro exists

Large advisory firms do good work, and they carry costs and constraints that don't always serve the
client. Recommendations get shaped by relationships the client can't see. The senior person who
won the work isn't the person doing it. Scope grows because growth is the business model.

SynPro is deliberately none of those things. It's a single senior advisor, with no supplier ties,
no software to sell, and no commission riding on the outcome. Fewer engagements, taken on properly,
by the person you actually met.

That's not a better model for every problem. For a client who needs a hundred consultants across
twelve countries, it isn't. For a client who needs one person with 25 years of judgement to get a
sourcing decision or a technology programme right, it usually is.

## Thought leadership

I wrote *The Critical Role of Data Governance in Corporate Real Estate* for the **Corporate Real
Estate Journal**, Vol. 13 (2024) — an argument that most workplace technology disappointments are
data problems wearing a software costume, which is a view the intervening years have not
contradicted.

I've spoken at **IFMA World Workplace** and at **TRIRIGA World**.
